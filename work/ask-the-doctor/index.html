Animating the science behind ABC’s Ask The Doctor

A library of 3D medical and scientific animation segments explaining sleep, metabolism, immune response and brain activity for a national television audience.

About Ask The Doctor

Ask The Doctor is an ABC iview factual series hosted by immunologist Associate Professor Shalin Naik, GP Dr Renee Lim and public health physician Dr Sandro Demaio. Each episode takes a real, common health question, such as how much sleep is enough, why diets fail, how the immune system actually fights infection, and unpacks the science behind it for a general television audience.

That premise depends on animation. The show is built around ideas that live inside the body: a famine-response hormone cycle, a swarm of immune cells converging on a threat, electrical activity moving across the brain, none of which a camera can film. Steps Medical was engaged to create a library of 3D medical and scientific animation segments woven through the series to carry exactly that part of the story.

The challenge

A television audience is broader and less captive than a clinical or investor one. The animation has to be immediately legible at a glance, hold attention inside a fast-moving broadcast segment, and still be scientifically accurate enough to sit next to two doctors and an immunologist explaining it on camera. Topics ranged widely from episode to episode: sleep physiology, the metabolic “famine response” behind yo-yo dieting, the immune system’s response inside a blood vessel, and neural activity in the brain, each needing its own visual language built from scratch.

Our approach

Each segment was designed as a short, self-contained visual explanation: a clean 3D scene with a single clear idea per shot, motion-graphics data callouts layered over the science (a “9 hours” sleep marker, a diet–famine response–fall off cycle diagram), and a consistent, broadcast-friendly colour and lighting treatment across every topic so the series feels like one show even as the science changes week to week. The immune-cell and brain sequences use full 3D rendering to give weight and realism to internal-body processes that would otherwise be flat diagrams.

What we delivered

  • A library of 3D medical and scientific animation segments spanning multiple episode topics
  • Motion-graphics data visualisation (sleep duration, metabolic cycle diagrams) layered into broadcast segments
  • 3D internal-body animation of immune response and neural activity for a general television audience

Where it’s used

The segments air as part of Ask The Doctor on ABC iview, appearing inside each episode at the moment the hosts explain the science behind that week’s health question.
